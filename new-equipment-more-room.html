Local gym updates fitness floor with new equipment and expanded classes

By Courier Staff 6 min read

By the time Mike Reynolds gets to Riverview Fitness after work, he usually has one thing on his mind: getting through his workout before dinner.

The 46-year-old electrician has belonged to gyms for most of his adult life. He likes familiar routines — free weights, a few resistance exercises, a short stretch, home. So when Riverview announced that it was updating its fitness floor, he paid attention.

“I’m pretty simple about what I want from a gym,” Reynolds said. “I want the equipment I use to be available, enough room that you’re not constantly working around somebody else, and somewhere I can get a decent workout in after work.”

Riverview, an independent community gym, completed the updates this spring after several months of member feedback.

The facility added several resistance machines, replaced older cardio equipment and reorganized the free-weight area. Additional cable stations now line one wall, and spacing around benches and squat racks has been increased.

The gym also expanded its class schedule, adding another evening strength class and weekend sessions.

Manager Elena Park said most of the changes came from watching how members used the facility.

“There were pieces of equipment that almost always had somebody waiting for them,” Park said. “There were also parts of the floor that weren’t being used efficiently. We wanted the space to work better when the gym was busiest.”

For Reynolds, one of the most noticeable changes has been an additional squat rack.

“There used to be nights where you’d finish half your workout before one opened up,” he said. “If you’re coming in after work and trying to get home for dinner, waiting around gets old pretty quickly.”

The new layout has also made it easier for him to move between exercises without changing his routine around crowded areas.

“I usually know what I’m doing before I get here,” Reynolds said. “I don’t want to spend a bunch of time figuring out Plan B because everything I wanted is taken.”

Jordan Lee, 31, has also been using Riverview for several years, although her routine looks different from Reynolds’s.

Lee divides her workouts between cardio equipment and group classes. On days when she exercises on her own, she usually starts on a treadmill before moving to a few resistance machines.

“I like having some structure, but I don’t want every workout to be exactly the same,” Lee said. “Sometimes I want to put my headphones on and do my own thing. Other days I’d rather take a class.”

The expanded evening schedule has made those classes easier for her to attend.

“There was one class I liked, but it started just early enough that I was always rushing to make it after work,” she said. “Now there’s another one later, so I can actually get there.”

Park said offering different kinds of workouts was part of the goal.

“Some members come in with a specific program and know exactly which rack or machine they want,” she said. “Others want a class, a treadmill or a few options depending on the day.”

Riverview also moved smaller equipment such as mats, kettlebells and resistance bands so members do not have to cross the gym to find what they need.

On weekday evenings, members move between machines and adjust weights while an instructor leads a class in the studio.

Reynolds said he does not need a gym to feel particularly new or elaborate.

“For me, it comes down to whether I can come in, do the workout I planned and not spend half the time waiting around,” he said.

Lee said her priorities are somewhat different.

“I want enough options that I don’t get bored,” she said. “If I can run, use some machines or take a class depending on what I feel like doing that day, that works for me.”

Park said that was ultimately the aim of the changes.

“We weren’t trying to turn Riverview into a completely different gym,” she said. “We wanted to take the things people already came here to do and make them a little easier to do.”

For Reynolds, that familiarity is part of why he has stayed.

“I know where everything is, I know what I’m doing, and I can get my workout done,” he said. “That’s really what I’m looking for.”
