Local gym redesigns changing spaces after hearing from members

By Courier Staff 6 min read

By the time Mike Reynolds gets to Riverview Fitness after work, he usually has one thing on his mind: getting through his workout before dinner.

The 46-year-old mechanic has belonged to gyms for most of his adult life. He likes familiar routines — free weights, a short stretch, home. So when Riverview announced that it was renovating its locker rooms and moving to a shared changing area with private rooms and showers, he paid attention.

“I’ll be straight with you, I wasn’t sure about it at first,” Reynolds said. “I grew up with the usual locker-room setup. That’s what I knew. When I heard we’d all be using the same shared changing area, I had questions.”

His concerns were practical, he said.

“Are we going to be changing in front of each other? What happens if somebody feels uncomfortable? What are the rules? I didn’t want asking those questions to be taken the wrong way. If we’re all using the same facility, I wanted to know how it was actually going to work.”

Riverview, an independent community gym, reopened the changing area this spring after several months of renovations and conversations with members.

The new space has a common locker area, but changing rooms and showers are fully enclosed and lock from the inside. Members are expected to be clothed or covered in the common area. The same rules about privacy, personal space and respectful conduct apply to everyone using the facility.

Staff have also received additional training on responding to harassment, conflicts and questions about the gym’s policies.

Near the changing area, a small sign carries a simple message: “We all train here.”

Manager Elena Park said the changes came from conversations with members who did not always experience fitness spaces in the same way.

“Some of us want more privacy than others. Some of us have been coming to gyms for decades, while others still walk in wondering whether they’re going to fit in,” Park said. “We don’t all bring the same history through the door. But we’re using the same facility, and we need the space to work for all of us.”

For Reynolds, seeing the space made a difference.

“I think I pictured something different in my head,” he said after a recent workout. “None of us is changing out in the open. I go into a private room, somebody else goes into another one, and then we come out and get on with our workouts. Once I understood that, most of what I was worried about wasn’t really an issue.”

Jordan Lee, 31, has also been using gyms for years.

“There are places where you walk in and immediately start wondering whether you’re the kind of person who fits there,” she said. “You notice who’s looking at you. You wonder if somebody thinks you’re out of place. Sometimes you’re doing all of that before you’ve even started your workout.”

At Riverview, Lee said, those questions tend to fade into the background.

“I don’t need everyone here to have the same life or think the same way I do,” she said. “I want to change, put my headphones on and work out. We’re all coming here for our own reasons, but we’re here because being active matters to us in some way.”

Park said that shared purpose is what Riverview has tried to emphasize.

“Saying that we’re one gym community doesn’t mean pretending we’re all the same,” she said. “We have different bodies, different backgrounds, different needs and different comfort levels. But when we come through these doors, this is our gym. We share the equipment, we share the changing space and we share some responsibility for making it a place where we can all get on with what we came here to do.”

Otherwise, little about Riverview feels unusual. The gym has rows of cardio machines, resistance equipment, free weights and a studio used for group classes. On weekday evenings, members move between sets, check their phones and wait for equipment to open up.

Park said that ordinariness is part of what the gym is trying to preserve.

“We’re here to move, get stronger, manage stress, feel better or look after our health,” she said. “We don’t all have to be the same. We just need to recognize that this is our space, and we’re sharing it.”
