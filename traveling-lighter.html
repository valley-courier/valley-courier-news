More travellers are packing lighter for short trips

By Courier Staff 6 min read

By the time Mike Reynolds started packing for a three-day trip last spring, his suitcase was already open on the bedroom floor.

The 46-year-old mechanic has never considered himself a light packer. Extra shoes and a few “just in case” items usually make their way into his luggage, even for a weekend.

“I’m the person who thinks, ‘What if it rains? What if we go somewhere nicer for dinner? What if I need another pair of shoes?’” Reynolds said. “Then suddenly I’ve packed for six days when I’m gone for three.”

So when he decided to make his most recent trip with only a carry-on bag, he was skeptical.

“I genuinely didn’t think everything was going to fit,” he said.

Local travel adviser Elena Park said more clients have been asking how to travel with smaller bags for short trips.

“For a lot of people, it’s less about becoming some expert minimalist traveller and more about making the trip easier,” Park said. “They don’t want to wait at baggage claim, keep track of multiple bags or bring things they never end up using.”

Park said the first step is usually deciding what the trip actually requires. A weekend built around sightseeing and casual meals needs a different packing list than a wedding, business trip or outdoor excursion.

“The easiest mistake is packing for every possible version of the trip instead of the trip you’re actually taking,” she said.

For Reynolds, that meant laying everything out before putting anything into the bag. He packed two pairs of pants, several shirts, one sweater and the shoes he planned to wear most of the weekend. A second pair of shoes took up nearly as much space as the rest of his clothing.

“That was the first thing I had to negotiate with myself,” he said. “Did I really need another pair? Usually I’d throw them in because there was room.”

He left them at home.

Reynolds also switched some of his usual toiletries into smaller containers and used packing cubes to keep clothing organized.

“You still have to make choices,” Reynolds said. “The cubes just make those choices look tidier.”

Jordan Lee, 31, has travelled with only a carry-on for several years. She said the habit developed after one trip when a checked bag arrived a day later than she did.

“After that, I started asking myself what I would actually miss if I didn’t pack it,” Lee said. “Usually the answer was not very much.”

Her approach is fairly repetitive. For short trips, she packs clothing that can be worn in more than one combination and chooses one jacket that works with everything. She keeps chargers and basic toiletries in the same travel pouch between trips.

“I’m not trying to win an award for packing the fewest things,” Lee said. “I just don’t like carrying a bunch of stuff around if I’m not going to use it.”

Park said one practical mistake is packing a carry-on so tightly that there is no room for anything picked up during the trip.

“You don’t need half an empty suitcase,” she said. “But if every zipper is under tension before you leave, you’ve probably made the return trip harder than it needs to be.”

Reynolds said his three-day trip did not turn him into a committed light packer, but it did change what he now considers necessary.

“There were a couple of moments where I thought, ‘It would have been nice to have that other shirt,’” he said. “But nice to have and needed are two different things.”

The biggest surprise came when he landed. Instead of waiting near the luggage carousel, he walked out of the airport with the same bag he had carried onto the plane.

“That part I liked immediately,” he said.

He has since used the same carry-on for another weekend away, although he admits an extra pair of shoes still occasionally makes the cut.

“I’m probably never going to be the person who travels for two weeks with one tiny backpack,” Reynolds said. “But for three or four days, I’ve figured out I need a lot less than I thought.”

Lee said that is roughly where she landed too.

“You learn your own version of enough,” she said. “Then you zip the bag, leave the house and stop thinking about it.”
